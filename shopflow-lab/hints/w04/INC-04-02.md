# INC-04-02: invoice off by one cent (RL-03)

Open **one level at a time**, and only after you have tried for about 10 minutes at the previous level.
Write down what you tried before opening the next level.

<details><summary>Level 1: the symptom, restated</summary>

Some invoices charge the customer one cent less than the sum of the lines. Prices like 0.50 or 2.00 are fine; 0.29, 0.57, 1.15 and 19.99 are not. Ten lines of 0.10 do not add up to exactly 1.00.

</details>

<details><summary>Level 2: where to look</summary>

Look at the type of the field in `Invoice` and at how `totalCents` converts it. Two suspects: the `double` and the cast to `long`.

</details>

<details><summary>Level 3: which tool to use</summary>

In a scratch `main`, print `0.29 * 100`, `0.1 + 0.1 + 0.1`, and `(long) (0.29 * 100)`. Compare with `new BigDecimal("0.29").multiply(new BigDecimal("100"))`.

</details>

<details><summary>Level 4: the concept to search for</summary>

Search for: *floating point rounding error 0.1 + 0.2*, *IEEE 754 double*, *Java BigDecimal vs double for money*, *BigDecimal(String) vs BigDecimal(double)*. Read Effective Java Item 60 (avoid float and double if exact answers are required).

</details>

<details><summary>Level 5: the shape of the fix (try to write it yourself first)</summary>

Keep the running total as a `BigDecimal`. `addLine` adds `unitPrice * quantity` using `BigDecimal` arithmetic only (never convert to double). `totalCents` moves the decimal point two places and rounds with an explicit `RoundingMode` before converting to `long`.

</details>
