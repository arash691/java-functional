# EX-08-03: DST gap and overlap: hint ladder

Open ONE level at a time, and only after you have tried for 10 minutes.

<details><summary>Level 1: symptom only</summary>

`resolve` and `laterOverlap` both start from a `LocalDateTime` (a wall-clock reading). The tests for 02:30 on 2025-03-09 and 01:30 on 2025-11-02 are the interesting ones. A wall-clock reading needs a zone before it means anything.
</details>

<details><summary>Level 2: where to look</summary>

`LocalDateTime` has a method that attaches a zone and returns a `ZonedDateTime`. `ZonedDateTime` has methods whose names mention "overlap".
</details>

<details><summary>Level 3: the tool</summary>

Run the worked example `TimeTour.main` and look at the line "02:30 in New York". Then print the same value for 01:30 on 2025-11-02. Write down the offset you see (`-04:00` or `-05:00`). The API docs of `ZonedDateTime.ofLocal` explain what happens in a gap and in an overlap.
</details>

<details><summary>Level 4: the concept to search</summary>

Search: "java ZonedDateTime withLaterOffsetAtOverlap" and "ZonedDateTime gap shift forward".
</details>

<details><summary>Level 5: fix outline</summary>

`resolve`: `local.atZone(zone)`. `laterOverlap`: the same, then call `.withLaterOffsetAtOverlap()`. For a time that is not in an overlap the call changes nothing.
</details>
