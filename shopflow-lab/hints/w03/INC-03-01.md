# INC-03-01: carts flagged as different (RL-01)

Open **one level at a time**, and only after you have tried for about 10 minutes at the previous level.
Write down what you tried before opening the next level.

<details><summary>Level 1: the symptom, restated</summary>

Two cart lines with quantity 5 are reported as the same, but two lines with quantity 128 or 1000 are reported as different. The guard test with 127 passes. The numbers 127 and 128 are not a coincidence.

</details>

<details><summary>Level 2: where to look</summary>

Open `LineChecker.sameQuantity`. Look at the *types* involved: what is the declared type of `CartLine.quantity()`? Compare it with the type of the `int` literal you pass in the test.

</details>

<details><summary>Level 3: which tool to use</summary>

Run the single test class and read the failing assertion message. Then, in the test or in a scratch `main`, print `Integer.valueOf(127) == Integer.valueOf(127)` and `Integer.valueOf(128) == Integer.valueOf(128)`. In IntelliJ, put a breakpoint in `sameQuantity` and look at the object ids of `a.quantity()` and `b.quantity()` in the debugger variables view.

</details>

<details><summary>Level 4: the concept to search for</summary>

Search for: *Java Integer cache -128 127*, *autoboxing*, *== vs equals for reference types*. Read Effective Java Item 61 (prefer primitive types to boxed primitives).

</details>

<details><summary>Level 5: the shape of the fix (try to write it yourself first)</summary>

Compare the two boxed values by value, not by identity. Either call `equals` on the boxed value, or unbox to `int` before comparing. Decide how you want to treat a missing (null) quantity and make sure it cannot crash the nightly job.

</details>
